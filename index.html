About Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan

Ustad Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan was a legendary Pakistani vocalist, widely regarded as one of the greatest voices in the history of Qawwali music.

He was born on October 13, 1948, in Faisalabad, Pakistan, into a family with a deep-rooted tradition in Qawwali spanning over 600 years.

Nusrat was known for his powerful voice, extraordinary vocal range, and ability to perform at a high intensity for several hours without losing energy or control.

He played a pivotal role in introducing Qawwali music to international audiences, blending classical Sufi devotion with modern music elements.

Collaborating with Western artists like Peter Gabriel and Eddie Vedder, he broke cultural barriers and expanded the global reach of South Asian music.

Throughout his career, Nusrat released over 125 albums and received numerous awards and accolades for his contributions to music and culture.

He was affectionately known as the "Shahenshah-e-Qawwali" (King of Kings of Qawwali).

His performances were not only musical experiences but also deeply spiritual journeys for listeners.

Ustad Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan passed away on August 16, 1997, in London, but his legacy continues to inspire musicians and fans around the world.

His music remains a timeless treasure, celebrated for its passion, spirituality, and universal appeal.
